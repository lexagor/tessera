Why security reviews stall

Logs are self-asserted

Anyone can edit a log. An enterprise reviewer cannot tell what the agent actually did, on whose authority, or whether the record changed.

Identity is not proof

IAM says which app an agent can reach. It does not bind the exact tool call, the human delegation, the policy version, and the outcome.

Deals get stuck

The buyer's security team asks for an audit trail and attribution. The vendor has dashboards and logs, but nothing a third party can verify.

Enforce, bind, verify, prove

  1. Enforce the declared intent against per-action policy and issue a scoped credential — or a broker-held grant the agent never sees.
  2. Bind the agent, delegating human, intent, policy hash, tool definition, arguments, credential thumbprint, and outcome into one signed record.
  3. Verify offline with the open-source verifier — no trust in the vendor, no call back to our servers.
  4. Prove it with a verification page and an exportable evidence pack mapped to SOC 2, ISO 42001, EU AI Act, FINRA, SEC 17a-4, and DORA.

Verification, not another dashboard

A receipt binds the authorization decision and the outcome into one signature. Anyone can recompute the hash, recover the signer, and check the chain.
